How it works

Open a folder. See the map. Ask.

You made an app with an AI and now can't find your way around it. WalkMyCode reads it the way a good lecturer would, then walks you through it at your pace.

Open a folder

Tell your AI app "open WalkMyCode on my project", or pick the folder on the start page. Several folders that make one system become one map.

“open WalkMyCode on ~/code/my-app”

See the map

Every page, route, function and table, in five lanes, with the real links between them, found in the code itself and checked against it.

Sign out → POST /account/signout → sessions

Ask, trace, take a tour

Click a card, select some lines, type or just say your question. The guide answers in plain English, points at real lines, and lights the map as it goes.

“how does sign-in work?”
What it does

A guide, not a search box.

Every answer points at the lines it came from. Click one and the code opens there.

▦A map by layer

Pages and components, API routes, logic, database, outside services. One app, a monorepo, or several services found from docker-compose and Kubernetes files.

PagesAPILogicDataOutside

⟶Trace it, end to end

From a button to the table it writes, hop by hop, each step with the line that proves it.

?Explain anything

A card, a file or a few selected lines: what it does and how it fits, for a beginner. Paste a screenshot of an error and ask about it.

▶Guided tours, read aloud

Ask for a tour of a feature: each step lights its lines and cards while the guide reads it out.

◉Talk to it

Tap Talk and ask out loud, as you would a person. Pause to think; it waits for the whole question.

◇Diagrams you can move

Ask for a diagram and get one drawn from the code: drag it, zoom it, open it full-window.

Install

Two minutes, and your own AI does the explaining.

WalkMyCode for Claude is free. The explaining runs on the AI app you already use, on your own plan: no API key, no account here.

Claude Code

Needs Node.js 20 or later.

  1. Add WalkMyCode once, for all your projects:
    claude mcp add -s user walkmycode -- npx -y walkmycode-mcp@latest
    On Windows:
    claude mcp add -s user walkmycode -- cmd /c npx -y walkmycode-mcp@latest
  2. In any project, ask Claude: “open WalkMyCode on this project”. The map opens in your browser.

Any other MCP app

Add a stdio server that runs this command, then ask your app to “open WalkMyCode on ~/code/my-app”. Your app's model does the explaining.

npx -y walkmycode-mcp@latest

From a terminal

No AI app? Run it on a folder: it maps it and opens the page, which answers with the AI key you add in its Settings (Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, Groq, DeepSeek, xAI, or a local Ollama or LM Studio).

npx walkmycode-mcp ~/code/my-app
What leaves your machine

Your code stays yours.

The map is built on your computer. Only what a question needs goes to the AI you chose, and nothing comes to us.

The mapBuilt by a parser on your computer. Nothing is uploaded to build it.
A questionThe question and the lines needed to answer it go to your AI app (Claude, on your plan), or to the one provider whose key you added. With a local model, nothing leaves at all.
Secrets.env files, *.pem and *.key are never read. Example config files are read for service addresses, with anything that looks like a password hidden.
VoiceOff unless you use it. Text read aloud and what you say go to the voice service you pick, on your key; your browser's own voice sends nothing anywhere.
The pageServed from 127.0.0.1 only, with a key in its link for each launch. Maps and answers are kept in ~/.walkmycode on your computer.
